The basics
Gum disease comes in two stages.
01GingivitisThe early stage. Gums look red or puffy, or bleed when you brush. With good daily care, it can usually be reversed.
02PeriodontitisCan follow if gingivitis is left for a long time. It affects the tissue that holds your teeth in place.

02
Good to know
If that sounds familiar, you’re far from alone.
Most adults in the UK have gum disease to some degree, and many don’t notice it.
NHS
03
Here’s the interesting part
Gum specialists and heart specialists looked at the research together.
In 2020, they found that people with gum disease are more likely to also have heart disease.
European Federation of Periodontology & World Heart Federation. Sanz et al., 2020
04
What that does and doesn’t mean
“A link,
not a cause.”
It doesn’t show that gum disease causes heart problems, or that treating your gums prevents them.
It’s simply one more good reason to look after both.

A gentle check-in
Any of these sound familiar?
Gums bleed when you brush or floss
Gums look red or puffy
Bad breath that lingers
Gums pulling back from your teeth
Comment your number. You’re in good company.

06
Every day
The daily easy wins.
01Brush twice a day for about two minutes, with fluoride toothpaste.
02Clean between your teeth once a day, with floss or small interdental brushes.
03After brushing, spit, don’t rinse, so the fluoride keeps working.
NHS
07
At the dentist
Three things worth asking for.
01Check-ups as often as your dentist recommends.
02A look at your gums, not just your teeth.
03If your gums often bleed, whether a hygienist visit would help.
