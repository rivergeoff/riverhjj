Symbolism

Meaning, in numbers

8

Rays of the sun

The sun of the Philippine flag, on a disc of the flag’s blue and red: a Filipino public school, and the light of learning.

2

Mountain peaks

A far-flung school in the uplands, and its two departments, elementary and secondary, integrated into one.

1

Hanging bridge

Strung between the peaks across the face of the sun, its deck on the line between blue and red: education reaches learners even in the mountains. Together we bridge.

1

Open book & river

A river runs from beneath the bridge down the valley and into the open book: from learning, the way leads up across the bridge and into the light.

4

Rice terraces

The four DepEd core values, Maka-Diyos, Maka-tao, Makakalikasan and Makabansa, built step by step in the valley.

3

Stars

The three stars of the flag: Luzon, Visayas and Mindanao.

Construction: 8 rays at 45° intervals around the sun; stars at 21° intervals; concentric rings at r 128 · 140 · 167 · 197 · 204 · 243.
Anatomy of the Seal

Built like an heirloom

Gold

The gold of Rosario, its mines and its livelihood, in the sun, the wreath, the terraces and every line of the seal.

The woven ring

A Cordillera-style diamond weave encircles the scene: the school’s Indigenous learners and heritage hold everything together.

The palay wreath

Two stalks of ripe grain rising from the foot of the seal: the harvest, and the community that the school feeds.

Motto & year

The motto in a calligraphic italic at the foot of the ring; the founding year beneath the scene.

Seal & name · Bold

Motto · Italic

Cormorant Garamond Italic, used only for the motto: a calligraphic voice beside the carved capitals.

Plus Jakarta Sans

Text · Labels & body

A clear contemporary sans for body copy, labels and small details such as “EST. 2012”.
